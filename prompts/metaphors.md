# ACT metaphors — menu

Name · (hexaflex processes) · one-line gist. Pick by process; retell in the week's terms.

## Acceptance and Willingness

- **Don’t Think About a Puppy** (acceptance, defusion) — Technician instructs client to avoid thinking about a puppy for several seconds. The exercise demonstrates that suppressing thoughts paradoxically increases their frequency and intensity, revealing the futility of thought control and the necessity of acceptance.
- **The Pendulum** (acceptance, self_as_context) — Technician presents a pendulum metaphor showing how fighting unwanted emotions amplifies them through the rebound principle. Client learns that willingness—moving from emotional control attempts to acceptance—allows natural emotional fluctuation without exhausting struggle or strengthening opposite states.
- **Ball in a Pool** (acceptance, defusion) — Technician presents struggle with unwanted thoughts and feelings as futile ball-pushing in water. Contrasts exhausting control attempts with acceptance: releasing the ball allows it to drift away naturally while freeing energy for valued living.
- **Room Full of Duct Tape** (acceptance, defusion) — A leaking pipe repaired repeatedly with duct tape gradually fills a room, leaving less space to live. The metaphor illustrates how avoidance strategies targeting unwanted thoughts and feelings create mounting costs, ultimately restricting life more than the original discomfort.
- **Building a House** (acceptance, committed_action, values) — Technician presents building a house using available bricks—some nice, some damaged—to illustrate how avoidance narrows life. Client explores trade-offs of experiential avoidance and considers constructing a larger life by accepting all internal experiences in service of valued living.
- **Life’s a Beach: Struggling in the Rip** (acceptance, defusion, present_moment, values, committed_action) — Technician guides client through a beach rip current scenario to illustrate struggling against psychological difficulties. Client learns that acceptance and values-guided action replace futile struggle, especially for anxiety, obsessions, and compulsive behaviors.
- **The Circus Act: Juggling and Hula Hoops** (acceptance) — Technician demonstrates how controlling thoughts and feelings seems manageable initially but becomes increasingly restrictive and unsustainable over time. Using props or gestures, the metaphor illustrates that avoidance strategies that appear harmless eventually collapse and constrain life.
- **Yes and No** (acceptance, present_moment) — Client practices saying 'no' to physical sensations for 2-3 minutes, then switches to saying 'yes' to the same sensations. This experiential contrast reveals the costs of experiential avoidance versus the ease of acceptance, teaching willingness through direct contact with resistance and openness.
- **Holding a Pen** (acceptance, committed_action) — Client physically grips a pen with maximum force, then gradually releases to a light hold while maintaining grasp. This teaches holding goals and efforts lightly—continuing valued action without desperate over-control—reducing frustration and exhaustion while staying committed.
- **Compassion** (acceptance, self_as_context, present_moment) — Therapist guides client through three sequential visualizations: experiencing compassion toward others, receiving compassion from others, then extending self-compassion. Targets difficulty accepting emotional pain and self-worth, particularly for those with trauma or shame.
- **Child** (acceptance, self_as_context, values) — Client revisits childhood self through guided imagery, meeting parents and adult self to ask for needed values like love or support. The exercise cultivates self-compassion and acceptance by allowing the present adult to offer what younger self needed, targeting internalized shame and self-abandonment.
- **Eating an Apple** (acceptance, present_moment, defusion) — Technician uses this metaphor to illustrate acceptance by contrasting struggling against experience (comparing apple to cupcake) with allowing experience as-is. Targets difficulty accepting life changes by teaching non-judgmental, present-moment engagement with what actually is.
- **Crying Baby on the Plane** (acceptance, defusion, present_moment) — A Technician describes sitting near a screaming baby on a plane to illustrate the difference between acceptance and desire. The metaphor teaches clients that willingness means allowing unwanted experiences without fighting them or needing them to change, while recognizing their temporary nature.
- **Understanding the Car** (acceptance, defusion, committed_action) — Technician presents a metaphor comparing intellectual understanding of how cars work to ruminating about feelings and thoughts. Client recognizes they drive without full comprehension, targeting the belief that understanding is necessary before acceptance and action toward valued goals.
- **Engaging the Clutch** (acceptance, defusion, present_moment, self_as_context, values, committed_action) — The mind is like a finicky engine; willingness and acceptance are the clutch that disengages unhelpful thoughts from behavior. Rather than fighting mental content, the client observes it mindfully while maintaining focus on valued direction and committed action.

## Cognitive Defusion

- **Floating Leaves on a Moving Stream** (defusion, present_moment, self_as_context, acceptance) — Client sits beside an imagined stream, observing thoughts as leaves floating past without interference. Teaches separation from thoughts and non-judgmental observation, reducing thought-fusion and reactivity.
- **Watching the Mind-Train** (defusion, self_as_context, acceptance, present_moment) — Client imagines standing on a bridge observing three trains carrying sensations, thoughts, and urges moving below. By staying on the bridge as an observer rather than boarding the trains, clients practice stepping back from fused thoughts and emotions, noticing when they get hooked into content.
- **Kicking Soccer Balls** (defusion, present_moment) — Client visualizes themselves on a familiar sports pitch, identifies distressing thoughts written on soccer balls, then physically kicks each ball away while watching it travel into the distance. This teaches cognitive defusion by creating distance between the self and intrusive thoughts, demonstrating that thoughts can be observed and released without engagement.
- **Boat on the Water** (acceptance, defusion, present_moment) — Client visualizes floating on a boat while waves rise and fall, observing thoughts and feelings as transient phenomena that naturally pass. Teaches that internal experiences, like waves, are temporary and can be experienced without control or struggle.
- **Pickle, Pickle, Pickle** (defusion, present_moment, self_as_context) — Technician guides client to repeatedly say a word (beginning with 'pickle,' progressing to personally difficult words) until it loses meaning and becomes an empty sound. Demonstrates how language creates psychological reactions and teaches that thoughts can be observed as separate from their content.
- **For S/he’s a Jolly Good ___________** (defusion, self_as_context) — Client sings a familiar tune while substituting a troublesome self-label (e.g., 'stalker,' 'failure') into the lyrics. Repetition and melody strip away the literal meaning and emotional charge of the fused thought, revealing it as just language rather than truth.
- **Say It in Another Language** (defusion, present_moment, self_as_context) — Client reads a thought in an unfamiliar language, then in English, noticing reduced emotional impact and behavioral pull from the foreign version. This demonstrates thoughts' arbitrariness and creates distance from their literal meaning, reducing automatic reactivity.
- **Don’t Do What You Say** (defusion, committed_action) — Client describes therapist's actions starting with "I must..." while simultaneously performing different gestures. Through ten repetitions, client directly experiences that language and behavior are separable, challenging the automatic link between thoughts and actions.
- **Name That Toon** (defusion, self_as_context, values, committed_action) — Client selects a cartoon character to voice their sticky thoughts, creating distance through humor and absurdity. This playful technique reduces thought credibility, revealing that thoughts don't require agreement or action, increasing choice about attention.
- **News of the World** (defusion, present_moment, acceptance) — Clients select newspaper headlines that trigger them, then read aloud while manipulating paper into musical instruments. As words blend into rhythm and sound, their emotional charge diminishes, demonstrating how defusion weakens thought power.
- **Brain Bingo** (defusion, self_as_context, acceptance) — Clients write negative thoughts onto index cards, populate bingo cards with these thoughts, then play bingo as thoughts are called aloud. The exercise teaches that thoughts are just words appearing and disappearing, not truths to struggle against, and can introduce the observer self holding all experiences.
- **Fly Fishing** (defusion, acceptance, values, committed_action) — The mind acts like a skilled fly fisher, casting thoughts and feelings designed to hook us. By recognizing these mental 'flies' without biting them, we reduce struggle and gain flexibility to swim toward our values.
- **Hands as Thoughts** (defusion, present_moment) — Client holds hands together before face, gradually raising them to cover eyes, then slowly lowering them. Demonstrates how fusion with thoughts narrows experience and restricts effective action, while defusion creates psychological distance, restoring contact with the present moment and flexible responding.
- **The Master Storyteller** (defusion, values, present_moment) — The mind continuously generates narratives—facts, opinions, predictions—seeking attention. Technicians help clients distinguish helpful from unhelpful stories and notice when thoughts pull them away from valued actions, rather than debating their truth.

## Present-Moment Awareness

- **Mindful Walking** (present_moment, committed_action) — Client walks with varied pacing and attention to physical sensations, noticing how body moves and where feet contact ground. Develops present-moment awareness through deliberate attention to walking mechanics, demonstrating capacity to choose how actions unfold.
- **Observing Thoughts** (present_moment, defusion, self_as_context, acceptance) — Client closes eyes and uses breath and body sensations as anchors while observing thoughts arise and pass without judgment. Develops capacity to notice thought characteristics (tone, speed, direction) and watch them like clouds, teaching defusion and present-moment awareness.
- **The Bag of Chips** (present_moment, acceptance, defusion, self_as_context) — Technician rustles a chip bag while clients listen with eyes closed. After opening their eyes, clients notice their automatic judgments about the neutral sound, revealing how interpretations create experience rather than sounds themselves.
- **Going Along with the Process** (present_moment, acceptance, defusion, self_as_context) — Clients focus on the breath cycle moment-by-moment with openness and curiosity, noticing each sensation from inhalation through exhalation. The breathing pattern becomes a metaphor for how any difficult process, including anxiety, unfolds naturally and can be observed rather than controlled.
- **Responding to Triggers** (present_moment, acceptance, defusion, committed_action) — Clients observe triggers (ice cream) and notice arising thoughts, feelings, and cravings without acting on them. Using the wave metaphor, they practice riding urges with breath, expanding around discomfort, and discovering that cravings naturally subside without control or suppression.
- **Blowing Bubbles** (present_moment, defusion, acceptance, self_as_context) — Clients blow and observe bubbles while practicing sustained attention to sensory details. Later, they imagine each bubble represents thoughts or feelings, practicing acceptance and cognitive defusion while noticing when attention drifts.
- **Value Parade** (present_moment, acceptance, self_as_context, values, committed_action) — Participants pair up to interview each other about difficult situations, writing each internal experience (thoughts, feelings, sensations) on name badges the speaker wears. They then silently walk around the room toward posted life domains while carrying these labeled private events, experiencing values-driven movement alongside pain.
- **The Dandelion** (acceptance, present_moment, defusion) — Client focuses on a painful physical or emotional sensation, visualizing it as a dandelion seedhead. Through rhythmic breathing, they observe seeds dispersing with each exhale, noticing discomfort's transient nature rather than fighting it. This cultivates acceptance and present-moment awareness of difficult internal experiences.
- **Willingness with an Avatar** (acceptance, defusion, present_moment, self_as_context, values, committed_action) — Client selects an object representing a difficult thought or feeling, then carries and interacts with it throughout the week in welcoming, caring ways. This builds a different relationship with internal experiences while practicing acceptance and present-moment awareness.
- **Music Mindfulness and Defusion** (present_moment, defusion, self_as_context, acceptance) — Client listens to music while observing present-moment sounds and bodily sensations, then notices thoughts, emotions, and judgments arising without attachment. This develops awareness of how the mind creates narratives and pulls attention from direct experience.

## Self-as-Context and Perspective Taking

- **Talking and Listening** (self_as_context, present_moment, defusion) — Client silently observes their own thoughts for thirty seconds, distinguishing the thinking self from the observing self. This experiential exercise builds awareness of the perspective from which thoughts can be witnessed, establishing groundwork for self-as-context.
- **Observing Self with Values** (self_as_context, present_moment, values, acceptance, defusion) — Clients write values on one side of a card and obstacles on the other, then use mindfulness and visualization to experientially access the observing self—the stable perspective that witnesses thoughts and feelings without being defined by them. This connects self-as-context awareness to values-directed living.
- **Fusion with Self-Evaluations** (defusion, self_as_context) — Therapist distinguishes between objective descriptions and subjective evaluations using a pen example, then applies this to client's self-talk. Client observes that negative self-statements are thoughts about the self, not facts inherent in the self, creating distance from fused self-evaluations.
- **I Can’t Possibly ___________** (defusion, self_as_context, values, committed_action) — Client speaks impossibilities while physically performing those actions (raising hand, hopping, clapping). Demonstrates that fusion with self-stories creates false limitations. Shows the gap between thought and behavior, enabling valued action despite anxious identity.
- **Conceptualized Self on Trial** (defusion, self_as_context, acceptance) — Clients generate identity statements that lawyers debate in a mock trial, with the client as judge and peers as jury. The jury must reach unanimous verdict on who the person "is." The impossibility of consensus teaches that identity is not fixed content—clients can observe thoughts and feelings without being defined by them.
- **The Classroom** (defusion, self_as_context, acceptance) — Technician presents a classroom scenario where students represent thoughts and feelings, the teacher represents the evaluating mind, and the classroom itself represents the observing self. Client learns to shift identity from content (thoughts/feelings/judgments) to context (the spacious awareness containing all experiences).
- **The Sky and the Weather** (self_as_context, defusion, acceptance) — Technician presents observing self as unchanging sky and thoughts/feelings as weather. This cultivates distance from mental events, normalizes their transience, and establishes a safe internal vantage point from which clients witness difficult experiences without being harmed.
- **The Prince and the Beggar** (self_as_context, defusion) — Technician tells story of identical prince and beggar who switch roles and clothes, yet each retains their core character. Teaches that sense of self persists beneath changing roles, circumstances, and internal states, anchoring clients in stable identity.
- **Taking Off Your Armor** (self_as_context, values, acceptance) — Technician helps trauma survivors recognize that defensive coping patterns once necessary no longer serve them. Explores how emotional armor that protected against hurt now prevents intimacy and connection, inviting clients to consider what becoming vulnerable might enable.
- **Muntu** (self_as_context, defusion, acceptance) — Technician describes muntu, a transcendent self in Kikongo culture that observes thoughts and feelings from a stable, unchanging vantage point. Clients learn to access their own observing self, separate from mental content, reducing attachment to outcomes.
- **The Anthropologist** (defusion, self_as_context, present_moment) — Client adopts observer perspective, studying internal experiences like thoughts and feelings with scientific curiosity rather than involvement. This stance creates distance from mental content, allowing non-judgmental awareness without fusion or reactivity.

## Values

- **The Prime-Time News Story** (values, present_moment, self_as_context) — A two-part guided visualization where clients imagine a biographical news story of their life today, then a follow-up story depicting their ideal future aligned with values. Comparing these narratives reveals gaps between current living and valued directions.
- **Mind-Reading Machine** (values, self_as_context) — Client imagines hearing thoughts from someone important to them through a hypothetical mind-reading machine, focusing on what that person would value about them. This clarifies personal values by exploring how the client wants to be perceived and what qualities matter most.
- **The Heroes** (values, committed_action, acceptance, present_moment, defusion, self_as_context) — Client identifies an admired person and lists their personal qualities, then explores how those qualities reflect their own values and life domains. This clarifies what matters most and reveals obstacles to values-consistent action.
- **The Sweet Spot** (values, present_moment, acceptance, committed_action) — Technician guides client to identify and vividly relive a moment of genuine sweetness or presence in their life, using either discussion or mindfulness. The exercise reveals values embedded in that experience and demonstrates how clients accept difficulties when aligned with what truly matters.
- **Writing Your Autobiography** (values, committed_action) — Client writes two autobiographies: one imagining an ideally lived life aligned with values, another reflecting current reality. Comparing these narratives reveals discrepancies between aspirational and actual living, clarifying values and directing committed action.
- **Ten Steps to Trying On a Value** (values, committed_action, defusion, acceptance, present_moment) — Clients experiment with behaving consistently with a chosen value for one to two weeks, tracking reactions and experiences in a daily diary. This experiential exercise helps clients discover personal meaning in values through direct contact rather than intellectual analysis, building willingness to commit to valued directions.
- **Table of Values** (values, committed_action, acceptance, defusion) — Technician and client collaboratively complete a structured worksheet mapping valued life directions to specific committed actions, identifying barriers, and selecting ACT-based strategies to manage obstacles. Makes values and committed action concrete and actionable.
- **Personal Job Ad** (values, committed_action, self_as_context) — Client writes a personal job ad describing their qualities, talents, values, ambitions, and non-negotiables—advertising themselves to the world rather than chasing external opportunities. This clarifies what truly matters and aligns life choices with core values.
- **The Scoreboard** (values, committed_action) — Technician uses a football game scenario to distinguish values from goals. Clients recognize that the meaningful aspect is playing the game itself, not just the final score, illustrating how values guide purposeful action beyond outcome attachment.
- **Remodeling the House** (values, committed_action) — Technician presents house foundation metaphor to illustrate that values form the essential basis for meaningful life choices. Client recognizes that aesthetic goals and actions (tiles, appliances, colors) lack stability without solid values underneath, teaching that values-aligned living provides integrity even when outcomes disappoint.
- **The Classroom Professor** (values, committed_action, acceptance) — A professor demonstrates jar-filling priorities using ping-pong balls (core values), rocks (important commitments), sand (daily tasks), and beer (balance). Technician uses this to help clients recognize when avoidance behaviors or minor tasks crowd out meaningful life priorities.
- **The Aerospace Engineer** (values, committed_action, defusion, acceptance) — Technician discusses an aerospace engineer balancing aircraft controls to illustrate maintaining equilibrium across multiple life values. Teaches that committed action requires focusing on controllable factors while accepting uncontrollable circumstances, preventing worry-driven fusion from derailing valued living.
- **The Cycling Race** (values, committed_action, defusion, acceptance) — Technician presents a metaphor of cyclists wearing shirts labeled with values, helping clients choose one primary value and take committed action toward it. Addresses how critical thoughts arise during valued action and teaches acceptance of those thoughts without compliance.

## Committed Action

- **Zorg the Alien** (defusion, values, committed_action, acceptance) — Technician guides client through an observational exercise using an alien character to assess the gap between current valued actions and desired ones. Client scores themselves across life domains, then explores what barriers (thoughts, feelings, avoidance) prevent movement toward valued living.
- **The Bubble** (acceptance, values, committed_action) — Technician guides client to visualize life as an expandable bubble that grows when accepting difficult experiences and contracts when avoiding them. Client tracks recent committed actions as vital or nonvital, noting whether each created expansion or contraction, building awareness of values-aligned behavior's life-expanding effects.
- **Bold Move** (values, committed_action, defusion, acceptance) — Therapist and client collaboratively brainstorm concrete action projects that express identified values. Projects must involve real behavior, not just thinking. Therapist helps client address obstacles using defusion and acceptance, encouraging bold moves despite mind's limiting thoughts.
- **The Traveling Partners** (acceptance, values, committed_action) — Client imagines a dream world trip but must choose whether to board a plane with undesirable fellow travelers. The metaphor illustrates how avoiding unwanted experiences (traveling partners) prevents full engagement with valued goals, teaching willingness and committed action despite discomfort.
- **Waiting for the Wrong Train** (acceptance, values, committed_action) — Technician presents a story about choosing between an uncomfortable train and a comfortable one that never leaves. Client recognizes how waiting for perfect conditions keeps them stuck and unable to move toward their values despite discomfort being necessary for meaningful progress.
- **The Rope Bridge** (acceptance, values, committed_action) — Technician presents a canyon-and-rope-bridge scenario where the client's valued goal lies across an unsafe bridge representing psychological barriers like fear and anxiety. Through exploring action choices—avoidance, reckless rushing, or cautious testing—the client recognizes that safety certainty requires movement, not paralysis.
- **Walking the Path** (acceptance, defusion, values, committed_action) — Technician guides client to observe the contrast between new, effortful values-aligned behaviors and familiar avoidant patterns. Client reflects on where old paths lead, then reconnects with the direction and rewards of the new path, building motivation for sustained behavioral change.
- **The Bicycle Factory** (committed_action, values) — Technician presents a factory with a manager overseeing production to illustrate ongoing therapeutic maintenance. Client discusses management tasks and consequences of abandonment, learning that psychological treatment requires persistent, flexible attention rather than one-time intervention.

## Bringing It All Together

- **The Sailing Boat** (acceptance, values, committed_action, defusion) — Technician guides client through a sailing metaphor showing how excessive bailing (problem-solving efforts) prevents directing the boat toward valued destinations. Highlights unworkability of controlling internal experiences and introduces values-based, committed action as an alternative path forward.
